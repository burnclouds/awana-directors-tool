# Blue Jewels follow the club quarter, not the book

**Blue Jewels are not earned by finishing a book.** The sources I could read describe them as an attendance award, usually about one per club quarter (roughly four per club year), and the attendance threshold varies by church. That means a book's start or finish date should not affect them under the model the sources describe. Book-completion jewels (red and green in the church materials) follow handbook sections, and the pilot wing holds 12 jewels in total. On the two harder questions, the evidence is thin. No source states whether a late finisher's next book counts from the day it opens, and no source addresses a book that spans two club years. The only cross-year practice that appears in more than one place is "a child who finished gets the next book; one who did not continues where they left off," and that rule appears only in single-church documents. None of this is official AWANA policy in the material I could access, and the local Sparks director remains the authority to confirm it.

## Blue Jewels are attendance-based and counted per quarter

The most consistent finding is that the blue jewel is a club-attendance award rather than a book award. A church Sparks letter says blue attendance jewels are awarded for each of the four quarters of the AWANA year, and a clubber who misses **two or fewer nights** in a quarter earns that quarter's jewel ([Sparks Parent Letter 23-24](https://www.springcreek.church/images/uploads/Sparks_Parent_Letter_23-24.pdf), search snippet). The same church guide says jewels are "determined by the Awana secretaries" from attendance and that red and green jewels come from completing book sections ([Sparks Parent Letter 24-25](https://www.springcreek.church/images/uploads/Awana%2024-25/Sparks%20Parent%20Letter%2024-25%20(website).pdf), search snippet). A different church states the bar as "not miss more than 2 weeks out of the quarter," and another says "usually attending 5-6 times/quarter" ([Sparks Info 2025-2026](https://storage2.snappages.site/F385K5/assets/files/2025-2026-Sparks-Info.pdf), search snippet). The thresholds therefore conflict across churches, and I could not verify any of them from a readable full document.

The pilot wing is the other place the question touches. The official AWANA store page confirms the wing "keeps 12 Achievement Jewels in place," with the three wings named HangGlider (green), WingRunner (blue), and SkyStormer (red) ([AWANA Store, Pilot Wing Badge](https://store.awana.org/product/pilot-wings-badge/), fetched and verified). That page does not link the jewels to attendance or to books. A 2026-2027 church letter describes 12 achievement jewels, four each of red (Bible drill sections), green (activity sections), and blue (club attendance), and says clubbers must pass all sections within each jewel section ([Sparks Letter to parents 2026](https://irp.cdn-website.com/117e96db/files/uploaded/Sparks+Letter+to+parents-2026.pdf), search snippet, PDF not extractable). Do not conflate the blue attendance jewel with the blue WingRunner wing; they are different items that share a color.

## Book timing: no source addresses the late-finish case

The question of whether a child who finishes a book on a late night starts the next book's count immediately or from its opening date has no documented answer. The official framing from Awana's own blog is pacing toward the end of the club year. Its 2024 post tells leaders to "work backward from how many sections they have to complete" so kids know how many to finish "between now and the end of the club year" ([Awana blog, 2024-02-28](https://www.awana.org/2024/02/28/3-tips-for-helping-kids-finish-their-handbooks/), fetched). That is a pacing instruction, not a jewel rule, and it says nothing about counting nights from a book's start.

Local practice is less clear. One Canadian church Sparks page says clubbers "can complete one book during one Awana year" and receive recognition as each section passes ([Thetapestry Sparks achievement page](https://sites.google.com/thetapestry.ca/awana/achievement-recognition), fetched, undated). That page also offers the Frequent Flyer extra-credit book to early finishers, which removes much of the pressure to start a new book mid-year. A search summary of Sparks family materials says book-completion awards are usually handed out at the end-of-year ceremony, with a mid-year finish announced and held for the ceremony. That claim comes from a PDF I could not read, so it is unverified. The most-cited local variant is to hold the child on the current book until the next Awana year, with review, and I found this in forum and leader-training snippets ([Westwood Sparks leader training](https://sites.google.com/view/westwood-awana/sparks/leader-training), 404 on fetch; [Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/490189-first-grader-halfway-through-his-awana-book), 404 on fetch). Neither page could be checked in full. The reading that best fits the evidence is that clubs treat the club year as the unit: a finish late in the year is a pacing problem, and a finish on the last nights does not start a new book's clock for jewels.

## A book that spans two club years

Here the evidence is clearest on the rule but weakest on the mechanics. A church family guide (2023-2024) and a 2025-2026 church handbook both say that a child in the same club who finished the prior book receives the next one, and a child who did not finish "continues where they left off" ([2023-2024 Family Guide](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c5f6bdd93a6326cafeffa2a/t/64d69c3a4b304d617e2bc944/1691786299068/2023+-+2024+Family+Guide.pdf), search snippet; [Awana Handbook for club year 2025-2026](https://storage2.snappages.site/N2DGD8/assets/files/Awana-Handbook-for-club-year-2025-2026-76.pdf), search snippet). Both are single-church documents, and I could not read either in full. A Sparks progression note describes a kindergartener working through Flight 3:16, HangGlider, WingRunner, and SkyStormer over three years, with books tied to grade path, though this too is an unverified summary.

No source says how nights, sections, or jewels are counted across the boundary, so a split count is undocumented rather than ruled out. The club-software picture does not settle it either. Approved Workman, a third-party club database, shows per-member book progress for the current club year and assigns the new club year by carrying the previous year's setup forward ([Approved Workman Club module](https://approvedworkman.freshdesk.com/solution/articles/16000021427-club-module); [Approved Workman, setup a new club year](https://approvedworkman.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/16000213066-setup-a-new-club-year)). That article says the uniform and current-handbook fields clear on promotion, but it does not say whether book progress or jewel history clears. The printed Cubbies achievement card, which travels with the child, is the other record-keeping model, but its Sparks equivalent is unverified for jewel handling.

## Evidence gaps and how to close them

The weakest part of this research is that almost every church-level claim comes from search snippets, and most PDFs (the 2024-25 and 2023-24 Spring Creek letters, the 2025-2026 Sparks Info, the 2026 letter, and the Parent Handbook) returned binary content on fetch. The AWANA awards packet is a 2018 edition and could not be read. The Sparks Curriculum Overview (2021) and the Awana Basics record-keeping module are behind a login or were not fetched. I found no official awana.org page that states the blue-jewel attendance rule, its threshold, excused absences, or makeup nights. No club secretary or leader's own account of late-finish or cross-year handling was found in public sources.

The rule to verify first is the attendance threshold. Four churches' materials disagree (two or fewer misses, two weeks out, 5-6 of 6 attendances), so one local handbook is not enough to call it standard. Ask the local Sparks director three things: whether blue jewels are attendance-based by quarter and what the cutoff is; whether a book finished on the final nights gets the next book's jewels immediately or from its opening; and whether an unfinished book carries into the next club year in the same club. Then ask the AWANA club-resource desk whether any official Sparks Leader document covers those points for the current year.

## Conclusion

The practical answer for a club is that Blue Jewels run on the calendar, one per quarter of attendance, and book timing does not move them. The open question that matters for leaders is the carry-over case, because a child who spans two club years is the one most likely to have a disputed count. The two sources most worth closing that gap are the 2025-2026 Sparks handbook used by the local club and a direct answer from the club's Awana secretary, not the church letters I could reach through search.
