# AWANA Blue Jewel and Book Completion: Local Club Practice (2026)

Status: partial. Most candidate sources returned search snippets only, or 403/404/binary PDFs that could not be read. Items marked "snippet only" were not verified against the full document.

## Key question 1: What do leaders say when a child finishes a book on the last nights?

### Takeaway
No retrieved source describes a specific rule for a book finished in the final weeks. The one fetched club page says a clubber "can complete one book during one Awana year," and awards are given as sections are passed. Late-year completion handling is not documented in any source I could read.

### Cited Findings
- "Clubbers can complete one book during one Awana year." Awana Thetapestry (Canada) Sparks achievement page, undated, fetched this session — https://sites.google.com/thetapestry.ca/awana/achievement-recognition
- "Children will receive achievement recognition when they pass section in the handbook." Same page — https://sites.google.com/thetapestry.ca/awana/achievement-recognition
- Awana's own blog advises leaders to "work backward from how many sections they have to complete" so kids "know how many they have to finish between now and the end of the club year." Post dated 2024-02-28 (first published 2018) — https://www.awana.org/2024/02/28/3-tips-for-helping-kids-finish-their-handbooks/
- Snippet only: a church Sparks guide says book-completion awards are typically given at the end-of-year ceremony, and a mid-year completion may be announced with the award held for the ceremony. Source: search-result summary of Sparks family materials (PDFs not readable; not verified).

### Inferences
- The official framing is "one book per club year, pace to the end of the year." Clubs treat the club year as the unit, so a late finish is a pacing problem in most written sources.
- The end-of-year awards ceremony appears to be the common place completed books are recognized.

### Gaps
- The 2025-2026 Sparks Info PDF, the Spring Creek Sparks parent letters (2023-24 and 2024-25), and the 2026 Sparks letter (irp.cdn-website.com) could not be read (404, or raw PDF with no extractable text). These are the likeliest local sources for late-finish handling.
- No source found on whether a child who finishes in the last week gets the next book's gems immediately or counts from the new book's opening.

## Key question 2: Do clubs carry a partly completed book into the next club year?

### Takeaway
One church family guide (snippet only) says an unfinished book resumes where the child stopped if they stay in the same club. Forum snippets describe a local rule that a child does not advance to the next book until the next Awana year. No source was read in full for this, and nights/gems counting across years is undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- Snippet only: a church family guide (2023-2024 Family Guide) says a child returning to the same club who completed the prior book receives the next one, and one who did not "will continue where they left off." PDF not readable; not verified — https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c5f6bdd93a6326cafeffa2a/t/64d69c3a4b304d617e2bc944/1691786299068/2023+-+2024+Family+Guide.pdf
- Snippet only: a Well-Trained Mind forum thread quotes a club saying "A student is not to move on to the next book until the next school year," with review-book practice. Thread page returned 404 on fetch — https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/490189-first-grader-halfway-through-his-awana-book
- Snippet only: a Sparks leader training page (Westwood) reportedly says a kindergartener who finishes HangGlider must review it before WingRunner and cannot start WingRunner until the next Awana year. Fetch returned 404 — https://sites.google.com/view/westwood-awana/sparks/leader-training
- Snippet only: a Sparks progression note says a kindergarten entrant works through Flight 3:16, HangGlider, WingRunner, and SkyStormer over three years, while a first grader completes Flight 3:16, WingRunner, and SkyStormer. Books are tied to grade/year path. Source: search-result summary; not verified.

### Inferences
- The reported practice is book-per-year: finish in the club year, or resume the same book next year in the same club. Split counting across years is not described.
- How nights and gems are counted for a resumed book is not documented in any retrieved source.

### Gaps
- No source states how nights, sections, or gems are counted across two club years.
- Parent-Handbook.pdf (snappages, two host IDs) was binary-only on fetch.

## Key question 3: Blue Jewels: per club year, or per book?

### Takeaway
Snippets consistently describe Blue Jewels as attendance-based and tied to quarters, not to finishing a book. The exact attendance threshold could not be verified from a readable source.

### Cited Findings
- Snippet only: a parent letter says blue attendance jewels are awarded for each of the 4 quarters, and a clubber may miss two times or less per quarter to receive the jewel. Source: search-result summary; PDF not fetched — https://www.springcreek.church/images/uploads/Sparks_Parent_Letter_23-24.pdf
- Snippet only: a church handbook says "a blue jewel is awarded for Club attendance," and a Sparks letter requires completion of red and green jewel sections plus the blue attendance requirement. Source: search-result summary; not verified.
- Snippet only: a church Sparks guide says a clubber is awarded a blue jewel for each quarter if they miss two or fewer times. Source: search-result summary; not verified.
- Snippet only: Sparks uses 12 achievement jewels (4 each of red, green, blue). Source: search-result summary; not verified.

### Inferences
- Under the local rules cited, Blue Jewels appear to be counted per quarter (about 4 per club year), so per club year rather than per book. This is inferred from several snippets agreeing on quarters; it is not confirmed from a readable primary document.
- The "miss two or fewer per quarter" threshold appears in more than one snippet with differing wording. Confirm against a current local handbook before relying on it.

### Gaps
- No readable source gives the official Blue Jewel attendance rule (nights per quarter, excused absences, makeup nights).
- No source explains whether a Blue Jewel is tied to a book's start date or the club year start.

## Key question 4: Common local variations

### Takeaway
Snippets show two local variations: (a) resume an unfinished book in the same club next year, and (b) hold a child on the current book until the next Awana year even after finishing, with review. The "count from book opening versus club year start" variation was not found in any source.

### Cited Findings
- Snippet only: "Some clubs hold children on the current book until the next Awana year." Source: forum and leader-training snippets; not verified in full.
- A child who finishes a handbook early may do the Frequent Flyer extra-credit book (extra credit pin). Verified on the Thetapestry page — https://sites.google.com/thetapestry.ca/awana/achievement-recognition

### Inferences
- Variation (b) is most likely what leaders mean by "the book doesn't move forward until next year."
- Frequent Flyer extra credit is a documented alternative for early finishers, which reduces pressure to start a new book mid-year.

### Gaps
- No source describes a club that counts book nights from the book's opening date. Ask the local club directly.

## Official vs. club practice
- Official (as far as found): Awana's published guidance is pacing toward the end of the club year (2024 Awana blog), and the Thetapestry page states one book per club year. Neither is a Blue Jewel rule.
- Club practice: carry-over, review-before-advance, and per-quarter attendance jewels appear only in church-level documents and forum posts, which vary by church.
